02 Approach

Problem first. Then the product.

Four steps, run in order, every time. The last one is the one most teams skip.

01

Find the real problem

Start from what people already struggle with and already work around. If nobody is improvising a fix today, it isn't a problem yet.

02

Name the constraint

Mobile money instead of cards. Expensive data. Mid-range Android phones. Regulators. We design for the conditions that exist, not the demo.

03

Build end to end

Product, interface, mobile and web clients, backend, payments and infrastructure — owned by one team, so nothing is lost between hand-offs.

04

Rebuild, don't defend

Put it in real hands. When it doesn't solve enough of the problem, we research again and rebuild it rather than marketing harder.

03 Capabilities

Everything it takes to ship.

A small stack, run lean on purpose: managed infrastructure, careful caching, and no service that needs babysitting.

Product research & strategy

User interviews, market sizing that is honest about the numbers, and positioning that follows from both.

Interface & design systems

Flows, high-fidelity screens and a component system that keeps web and mobile consistent as the product grows.

Mobile & web apps

One React Native and Expo codebase for iOS, Android and the web — offline-first, with background sync.

Backend & data

Postgres with row-level security on every table, edge functions for anything that holds a key, and webhooks for async work.

Applied AI

Generation that is the product, not a bolt-on — with server-side keys, per-user quotas and cost engineering built in.

Payments

Mobile money and local cards through Paystack, international cards through Stripe, subscriptions and billing portals.

Localisation

Products that launch in nine languages from one codebase, priced in the currency and cycle people actually live on.

Security & operations

Abuse controls, access reviews and deploy discipline — the unglamorous work that keeps a live product trustworthy.

04 How we think

  • Constraints are the brief.

    A student without a Visa card is a customer, not a lost signup. A phone on an expensive data plan is the target device, not an edge case. The constraint tells you what to build.

  • Cost is a design decision.

    If a feature has a marginal cost, its economics and its user experience are the same conversation. We design both at once, so a generous free tier is sustainable.

  • Trust is built in the details.

    Itemised fees, keys that never reach the client, row-level security everywhere. People can't see most of it, but they can tell when it's missing.

  • Small teams, whole products.

    One team owning research, design, engineering and operations moves faster and loses less in translation than a relay of specialists.
