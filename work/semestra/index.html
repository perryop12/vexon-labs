01 The problem

Every student is handed the raw material. Almost none convert it.

A semester arrives as a course outline, a folder of slide decks, some scanned readings and a timetable. The work of turning that into revision — summarising, writing questions, spacing it out — is the part that actually produces a grade. It's also the part that gets skipped.

The tools students already have each solve a slice of it, and none of them own the whole semester:

Planners

Organise, but don't teach

Calendars and to-do apps track when the exam is. They do nothing about whether you understand the material.

General AI chat

Answers, but no structure

A chatbot will explain a concept on request. It doesn't know the course, doesn't build a curriculum, and doesn't come back in four days to check retention.

The gap

Nothing owns the semester

The student is left to be their own instructional designer — every week, across five courses.

02 How it works

Three steps. The third is the one that compounds.

Step 01

Bring the material you already have.

Semestra works from what the lecturer actually handed out. Several sources can be combined into a single lesson, and a YouTube lecture is read through its transcript.

PDFWordPowerPointPlain text & MarkdownPhotos of notesWeb linksYouTube lectures
Step 02

Get a lesson, not a summary.

The source becomes a readable lesson with learning objectives and concept sections, a quiz drawn from that specific material, and a flashcard deck. Every question is answerable from what the student was given — not from a generic question bank — and each one comes with an explanation.

Step 03

The schedule brings it back.

Quiz results feed an SM-2 spaced-repetition schedule, so weak concepts resurface before they fade. Reminders land against the student's real class times — not a generic 9am nudge — and "30 concepts due for review" sits right next to today's lectures.

05 Why AI-native

Remove the model and there is no product left.

Semestra isn't a planner with a chatbot bolted on. Generation is the core loop: every lesson, every quiz question, every flashcard and every audio track is produced at request time from a document the student supplied minutes earlier.

Gemini Flash
Lesson & quiz generation

Structured lesson content, multiple-choice questions with explanations, and flashcard decks — produced from arbitrary uploaded documents.

Gemini TTS
Natural-voice lesson audio

Any lesson becomes something a student can listen to. Audio is cached after the first synthesis, so a replay costs nothing.

Cost engineering
A cross-user lesson cache

Lessons are keyed by the SHA-256 of the source document. When forty students on one course upload the same outline, it's generated once and served forty times — and the file itself is never stored.

Design decision

Caching by content hash does double duty: it cuts the cost of the most expensive operation in the product, and it means Semestra never has to keep a student's source files. The cheaper path and the more private path are the same path.

06 Built for this market

Built in Accra, for the constraints students here actually have.

The African university student is not an edge case to localise for later. Every decision below came from the market Semestra launched in.

  1. Mobile money, not just cards

    Paystack handles Ghanaian mobile money and local cards; Stripe handles international. A student without a Visa card is a customer, not a lost signup.

  2. An ad-funded free tier

    Free users get four courses and four AI lessons a month, and can watch a rewarded ad to unlock a quiz. Ability to pay is not the gate on studying.

  3. Large classes, few contact hours

    Where one lecturer serves several hundred students, per-student explanation has to come from somewhere. That is precisely what generation is good at.

  4. Nine languages, one codebase

    English, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Spanish, German, Italian, Hindi and Chinese — covering most of the continent's languages of instruction from launch.

07 Business model

Free to study. Paid to study faster.

Priced against a student's budget in each market rather than converted from a dollar figure. Premium unlocks unlimited AI lessons, quizzes, flashcards, lesson audio and cross-device sync. New accounts get seven days of Premium free, no card required.

08 Under the hood

A small stack, run lean on purpose.

Client

Expo · React Native

  • One codebase for iOS, Android and web
  • Offline-first local storage
  • Background sync across devices
  • Feature parity incl. groups and payments
Backend

Supabase · Postgres

  • Row-level security on every table
  • Edge Functions for anything holding a key
  • Database webhooks for async work
  • Paystack + Stripe billing
Intelligence

Gemini, server-side only

  • No model key ever reaches a client
  • Generation, grading and speech behind Edge Functions
  • Per-user quota enforcement
  • Content-hash lesson cache

Everything above is operated by one person. That constraint shaped the architecture: managed infrastructure, aggressive caching, and no service that needs babysitting.

Timing
Revenue starts with the semester, not the calendar

Student software is bought when a term begins. The first version didn't solve enough of the problem to charge for, so it was rebuilt after more research rather than pushed into the mid-year trough — a window that would have tested nothing. Both payment rails are live; September is the first real commercial test.

The rebuild
Willing to rebuild rather than defend

Version one shipped, met real students, and fell short. Instead of marketing harder, it was researched again and substantially rebuilt — payments, study groups, spaced repetition, lesson audio and nine languages — before opening the paid tier.

10 What we learned

The student already had the material. What they were missing was someone to turn it into studying — and to come back for it four days later.

  1. Own the loop, not the feature

    Lessons alone are a summariser. Spaced review tied to the real timetable is what turns one upload into a semester of retention — and it's the part competitors don't have.

  2. Price in the unit people think in

    Students think in semesters and cash cycles, not calendar years. A semester plan and mobile money did more for access than any discount.

  3. Cost engineering is product design

    A content-hash cache and per-user quotas are what make a generous free tier possible in a price-sensitive market.

  4. Rebuild when the evidence says so

    A product that doesn't solve enough of the problem won't be saved by marketing. Going back to research was the fastest route to something worth paying for.
